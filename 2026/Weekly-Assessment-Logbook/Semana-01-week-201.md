### Weekly Assessment
This was a useful but overfilled absorbent re-entry week. The weekly skeleton held: the two key aerobic rides were completed, the easy Wednesday sat in the right place, and the back half of the week remained free of formal training. But the intended absorbent shape was only partially preserved because total volume still rose above the planned reference and above the 5-6 hour target band, while the final three rest days accumulated substantial walking load. The result is a week that restored continuity and ended much fresher than it began, but still behaved more like maintenance with excessive drift than like a clean absorption microcycle.

### Evidence
- Plan: Week 201 in block B2 was designed as an absorbent re-entry week. Its primary role was to restore functional margin while keeping the bike as the main axis, with a 5.0-6.0 hour target volume band. The key sessions were a stable aerobic reference ride on Tuesday and a second useful Z2 ride on Thursday; the rest of the week was supposed to protect absorption, especially the Friday-to-Sunday travel stretch.
- Execution: Adherence was 85.71% with 100% traceability. Six days closed as completed and one as modified. Actual time reached 450 minutes against 370 planned reference minutes, a delta of +80 minutes, and it also sat 90-150 minutes above the stated weekly volume band. Monday opened with a contained 101-minute recovery ride plus 31 minutes of strength, Tuesday completed the key 120-minute Z2 reference ride plus 29 minutes of strength, Wednesday kept the easy walk role, Thursday completed the second key Z2 ride at 127 minutes, and Friday to Sunday stayed free of formal sessions. The main execution drift was not missed key work but excess incidental volume on the three rest days and two unrecorded or skipped complementary strength/core pieces on Wednesday and Thursday.
- Physiology: The pattern was mixed but mostly supportive. Sleep was acceptable on most recorded days at 7.27, 7.88, 7.28, 8.53, and 7.88 hours, with Tuesday lower at 6.45 hours and Friday missing. Resting heart rate stayed low through the training days at 40-46 bpm, rose to 51 on Friday, then normalized again at 46-47 over the weekend. Average stress was low to moderate on most days, but Friday rose to 58, which fits the idea that the first travel rest day was not especially restful in practice. There is still no HRV, body battery, or consistent subjective reporting, so the physiology read is directionally useful but incomplete.
- Load: Load distribution is the decisive weekly issue. Monday was appropriately contained at 83.81 daily load despite over two hours of total work. Tuesday rose to 132.26 and Thursday to 142.60, both productive but expensive inside a week meant to absorb. Wednesday was the only true easing day at 25.62, which improved TSB from -40.60 to -30.83, but Thursday largely gave that relief back by returning TSB to -36.47. The week then closed with three zero-load days in the model, which brought ATL down from 96.70 on Thursday to 60.90 on Sunday and TSB up from -36.47 to -4.86. So freshness did recover by the end, but only after a week that remained denser than its role called for.
- Zones: The weekly zone-coherence assessment is coherent. Heart-rate evidence showed two aligned cycling sessions, one misaligned session that still looked terrain- and execution-driven rather than profile-driven, and two limited comparisons. The active heart-rate profile remains the 2026-06-02 five-zone reserve model with Z2 capped at 137 bpm, and there were no actionable proposals to change it. Power has no useful comparable evidence this week. This supports keeping the current zones rather than revising them now.
- Day Pattern: Week Control: The intended shape mostly held in sequence, with recovery on Monday, key aerobic work on Tuesday and Thursday, and rest scheduled for the final three days. The problem is that the week stayed too full for its absorbent purpose: actual minutes overshot reference, Thursday was costlier than the week role ideally wanted, and Friday did not become true rest in practice. Load And Recovery Distribution: Load concentrated on Tuesday and Thursday, while Wednesday only partially absorbed it. The three modeled zero-load days finally restored freshness, but Friday's very high walking volume delayed the absorption effect that the plan wanted to start immediately. Decisive Days: Tuesday mattered because it proved the aerobic reference session could be completed with good control even under fatigue. Thursday mattered more because it was efficient and durable but too expensive for the week's role, effectively reloading the fatigue stack. Friday is the day that most changed the weekly meaning: instead of becoming true travel rest, it accumulated 22,432 steps and 19.1 km on foot. Across Friday to Sunday, the rest block still totaled 49,794 steps and 42.4 km, so the week's closing rest was active rather than passive. Zone Coherence: The current zone definitions still look coherent, and the one misaligned cycling session is better explained by terrain and session shape than by stale thresholds.

### Coaching Interpretation
- What went well: The week preserved structure, both key aerobic rides were completed well, the Tuesday reference ride and Thursday endurance ride were technically sound, and the back end of the week eventually restored freshness to near neutral. Traceability was complete, and there is no evidence of chaotic or off-plan intensity.
- What is concerning: The week was too heavy for an absorbent re-entry role. Volume overshot both the weekly reference and the stated volume band, Wednesday did not buy enough relief before Thursday, and the Friday-to-Sunday rest block carried far more walking than a true recovery close. Missing HRV, body battery, and subjective data reduce certainty about how costly that drift felt internally.
- Whether the week should be read as progression, maintenance, recovery, overload, or under-target: Excessive week for its intended role. It was a useful maintenance week in training quality terms, but excessive relative to the absorbent control the microcycle was supposed to preserve.

### Decision
- Recommendation for how to treat the next week's plan: Keep the basic structure of week 202, but do not try to hit the full 11-13 hour construction target from day one. Hold Monday strictly easy, keep Tuesday and Thursday at the low end of their prescribed ranges unless sensations are clearly good, and be ready to trim Sunday or even Saturday volume if freshness does not stay stable after the first half of the week. In practice, this means opening week 202 as controlled progression rather than as a full normal-load jump.
- Zone-definition recommendation: Keep current zones.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Missing data that could change the conclusion: HRV, body battery, reliable day-by-day subjective freshness, confirmation of whether the omitted core and upper-body sessions were intentionally skipped or simply unrecorded, and a cleaner recovery signal for the Friday travel day.
